      Webcam eye tracking · no compromises

      Appearance nets on the GPU,
      a 3D ray to the screen

      An ensemble of ETH-XGaze-trained gaze networks (UniGaze ViT-H, PureGaze, GazeTR, ResNet-18) predicts a 3D gaze direction from a head-pose-normalised face crop. A PnP head pose turns that into a ray from your face, intersected with the physical screen plane, then a short person-specific calibration fits the residual geometry — so the mapping survives head movement instead of memorising one pose.

      Inference runs in a local Python process on Apple Silicon (Metal). First start loads ~3 GB of weights.

      Phase 01

      Sit the way you will use it,
      then calibrate

      1. Sit at your normal distance. Depth comes from the face model and the camera's field of view; the calibration's head-motion targets refine that scale, so nothing has to be typed.
      2. Front light, no Center Stage. Turn off Center Stage in Control Center — it crops the camera dynamically and would silently change the intrinsics mid-run.
      3. Head motion is welcome. Some calibration targets ask you to keep fixating while turning and leaning — that is what teaches the mapping to hold up when you move later.

      Before calibration the marker uses the prior geometry only — camera at top centre, no personal offset. Expect it to be off by a few degrees.
